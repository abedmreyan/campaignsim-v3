COCA-COLA "SHARE A COKE" (AUSTRALIA) — CAMPAIGN BRIEF
Prepared for: Marketing Campaign Simulation Validation
Source: Published case studies (Ogilvy Sydney, Coca-Cola press materials, marketing case study coverage, 2011 Australian launch)

=== BRAND OVERVIEW ===

Coca-Cola is the world's best-known soft drink brand. By 2011, Coca-Cola's home market of Australia had seen more than a decade of slowly declining soda consumption, especially among young adults shifting toward other beverage categories. Coca-Cola South Pacific, working with agency Ogilvy Sydney, needed a campaign that would make the brand feel personal and relevant again to a generation that increasingly saw mass-market soda as generic and impersonal.

Brand values: Joy, sharing, togetherness, optimism.
Market positioning: Coca-Cola isn't just a drink — it's something you share with someone you know by name.

=== THE CAMPAIGN MECHANIC ===

Coca-Cola replaced its iconic logo on bottles and cans with the 150 most popular Australian first names ("Share a Coke with Jack," "Share a Coke with Mia," etc.), turning a mass-produced product into something that felt personalized. In-store kiosks and an online "name generator" let people order a personalized can with a name not in the initial 150, and consumers were encouraged to photograph and share their personalized bottle on social media.

=== TARGET AUDIENCE SEGMENTS ===

Segment 1 — The Disengaged Young Adult
Age: 18–29.
Psychographics: Grew up with Coca-Cola but increasingly sees it as their parents' drink. Drinking less soda overall, more brand-agnostic about beverage choice.
Platforms: Facebook, Instagram, in-person social occasions (parties, gatherings, summer outings).
Buying behaviour: Buys on impulse and occasion rather than habitual loyalty. Influenced heavily by what feels "shareable" or social-media-worthy.
Ad response: Unmoved by traditional brand advertising. Highly responsive to anything personalized, novel, or built for sharing with friends.

Segment 2 — The Family Shopper
Age: 30–50.
Psychographics: Buys soft drinks for household/family consumption, shops primarily at major supermarkets, values a sense of fun for family occasions.
Platforms: TV, in-store point-of-sale, Facebook.
Buying behaviour: Habitual category buyer, but switches between brands based on in-store prompts and family preference.
Ad response: Responds to in-store novelty and to content that fits family or gift-giving occasions (e.g., finding a child's or relative's name on a bottle).

=== CAMPAIGN OBJECTIVE ===

Objective: Reverse declining Coca-Cola consumption among young adults in Australia and generate a wave of organic social sharing around the brand.

Context: This was treated as a one-summer activation (Australian summer, Nov 2011–Jan 2012) to test whether mass personalization could outperform standard seasonal advertising.

Target metric: Year-over-year volume sales growth during the campaign period, and social media engagement (UGC posts, follower growth) generated by the personalized packaging.

=== CAMPAIGN VARIANTS TO TEST ===

VARIANT A — "Find Your Name" (retail reminder, email)
Headline: Jack, Mia, Sam... is your name on a Coke yet?
Body: Find your name, or the name of someone you love, on a bottle of Coke at your local store this week. Buy it for them, not just for yourself.
CTA: Find your name in store
Target segment: Family Shopper (Segment 2)
Channel: Email
Format: EmailNewsletter

VARIANT B — "Name Wall" (personalization showcase)
Headline: 150 names. One of them is yours.
Body: Swipe through the names already on shelves — Jack, Mia, Sam, and 147 more. Didn't find yours? Make one at the kiosk.
CTA: See if your name made the cut
Target segment: Disengaged Young Adult (Segment 1)
Channel: Instagram
Format: CarouselPost

VARIANT C — "Make Your Own" (UGC sharing trend)
Headline: Didn't find your name? Make it yourself.
Body: Personalize a can at the kiosk or name generator, then show it off. Tag #ShareACoke and see how many other names are out there.
CTA: Personalize your Coke
Target segment: Disengaged Young Adult (Segment 1)
Channel: TikTok
Format: ShortFormVideo

=== BRAND VOICE ===

Tone: Warm, personal, optimistic, inclusive.
We talk about individual people and relationships, not abstract brand messaging — the product itself becomes the message.
