# CampaignSim — Brand & Visual Identity Guide

**Version:** 2.0  
**Last Updated:** 2026-06-04

## Introduction

This document defines the complete visual and brand identity for CampaignSim. It exists to ensure that every surface — from the platform UI to a thesis presentation slide to a future product landing page — communicates a single, coherent story about what this platform is, who it's for, and why it matters.

CampaignSim is an AI-powered marketing simulation platform. It allows marketers to build a knowledge graph of their brand, generate audience personas, and run predictive simulations of campaign performance — before a single dollar is spent. The brand must carry that promise: **intelligence ahead of investment, signal before spend.**

---

## 1. Brand Identity

### 1.1 Brand Story

Every marketing team knows the anxiety that comes before a campaign launch. The budget is approved, the creative is signed off, the targeting is set — and then you wait. You wait to see if the message lands. You wait to find out if the audience actually cares. You wait for the data to come back, and by then, the money is already gone.

CampaignSim was built to end that waiting. It gives marketing teams the ability to simulate what happens before it happens — to run hundreds of virtual audience members through a campaign, watch how they respond, and understand why. The knowledge graph knows the relationships. The personas carry the context. The simulation engine runs the scenario. The report tells you what it means.

This is not a reporting tool. It is not an analytics dashboard. It is a **prediction engine for human attention** — and the brand must look and feel like it belongs in that category.

### 1.2 Market & Regional Context

CampaignSim is designed for the modern MarTech environment, with particular relevance to the **GCC and MENA region**, where digital marketing investment is accelerating rapidly. Saudi Vision 2030 and the UAE's digital economy strategies have created a generation of brand managers, growth marketers, and digital strategists who are sophisticated consumers of marketing technology — but who are also underserved by tools built for Western markets that don't account for the region's audience dynamics, platform mix, or cultural nuance.

The global MarTech landscape is dominated by platforms that emphasize dashboards and retrospective reporting (Google Analytics, Meta Ads Manager, HubSpot). CampaignSim occupies a different and currently underpopulated space: **pre-launch predictive simulation**. The brand must communicate this difference clearly. It should not look like another analytics platform. It should look like the platform that comes before all the others.

The target user is a marketing strategist, brand manager, or digital campaign lead at a mid-to-large brand or agency. They are analytically literate, time-pressured, and increasingly aware that AI tools are available — but skeptical of platforms that don't demonstrate real intelligence.

### 1.3 Brand Personality

| Trait | What It Means in Practice |
|---|---|
| **Precise** | Every design decision is intentional. No decorative elements that don't carry meaning. Typography is tight. Color usage is disciplined. |
| **Authoritative** | The platform makes confident predictions. The brand should reinforce that confidence — not hedge it. Headlines are declarative. Layouts are structured. |
| **Forward-facing** | This tool looks ahead, not back. The visual language should feel like it's oriented toward the future — movement, signal, trajectory. |
| **Grounded** | Despite being AI-powered, the platform is practical and serious. It is a professional tool, not a tech toy. It earns trust through credibility, not novelty. |
| **Regionally Aware** | The brand does not import a Western tech-startup aesthetic wholesale. It draws from international standards while carrying visual signals that resonate in the MENA context — premium, clean, and sophisticated. |

### 1.4 Brand Voice

- **Headlines:** Short, declarative, confident. "Run the simulation first." Not "Leverage our AI-powered platform to optimize your pre-launch strategy."
- **UI copy:** Precise and functional. Tell the user what something is. Never use filler words.
- **Reports:** Analytical and structured — written like a strategic brief, not a marketing presentation.
- **Error messages:** Honest and direct. Not apologetic. Not playful.

### 1.5 Tagline

**"Simulate the signal."**

This line works because it captures the core action (simulate) and the core output (a campaign signal — a message that propagates through an audience). It is short enough for a logo lockup, meaningful enough for a thesis abstract, and memorable enough for a product pitch. It uses language from both marketing (signal, campaign) and engineering (simulate), bridging the two worlds the product inhabits.

## 2. Color System

### 2.1 Color Philosophy & Justification

Color choices for CampaignSim are grounded in three considerations: **industry positioning**, **regional market context**, and **functional clarity within a dark-themed data platform**.

#### 2.1.1 Competitive Color Landscape Analysis

Before selecting any color, the dominant color territories in the software market were mapped and evaluated for fit. Each major color family carries established brand ownership and audience associations that either align with or conflict with CampaignSim's positioning.

**Cold Blue — rejected**  
Deep navy and cold blue (`#003`, `#0052CC`, `#1E40AF` range) are owned by enterprise software. Salesforce, SAP, LinkedIn, IBM, and Jira all live in this territory. The association is stability and institutional trust — appropriate for ERP and CRM tools, but incorrect for a predictive simulation platform. Cold blue makes a product feel like it came from 2010.

**Purple / Violet / Indigo — rejected**  
The purple-to-indigo range has been colonized by two adjacent categories: creative tools (Figma, Canva, Notion, Linear) and "AI startup" branding (countless LLM wrappers launched between 2022–2025 have defaulted to indigo gradients on dark backgrounds). The category is visually saturated to the point of being a liability. A new platform launching in this color space would appear derivative and blend into the noise rather than differentiate from it. Beyond positioning, purple and violet carry no functional meaning in the data/analytics context — they have no semantic association with intelligence, precision, or campaign performance.

**Warm Orange as Primary — rejected**  
Warm orange (`#FF6B00`, `#E8561E` range) at a primary brand level reads as consumer-facing product (Fanta, Duolingo, DoorDash, Swiggy) or early-stage startup. In the GCC market specifically, aggressive orange is used extensively in telecom and fast-moving consumer goods — it carries high energy but low credibility for professional analytics tools. Orange can work as a CTA accent (HubSpot uses it this way), but it should not be the primary brand identity of a platform designed for data-driven marketing professionals.

**Red — rejected**  
Red is entirely occupied by danger, alert, and error semantics within any UI system. Using it as a brand color creates constant visual conflict with error states and is inappropriate for a platform whose outputs should feel positive and actionable.

**The resulting conclusion:** The correct color territory for CampaignSim sits in the teal-to-green spectrum — the one major family not yet owned by any dominant category in the MarTech simulation space, carrying functional associations (data intelligence, growth, precision) that are directly relevant to the product, and resonating culturally in the primary target market.

---

Instead, the palette is built around **teal as the primary intelligence color**, **emerald green as the growth and success signal**, and **amber gold as the premium data accent**. Each of these choices is justified below.

---

### 2.2 Primary Palette

#### Signal Teal — `#0ABFAD`

**RGB:** 10, 191, 173  
**HSL:** 175°, 90%, 39%

Teal is the color of analytical intelligence in the global MarTech landscape. It is used as a primary or key accent by Intercom, Zendesk, Atlassian, and — notably — Aether: Foundation, one of the region's own emerging platforms. Its prevalence in the data and analytics category is not coincidental: teal sits between blue (trust, structure) and green (growth, action), which makes it the instinctive choice for tools that combine data authority with forward motion.

For CampaignSim specifically, teal signals **signal clarity** — the idea that the platform cuts through noise to give you a clean, precise read on what will happen. It reads as intelligent without being cold, and modern without being trendy.

**Usage:** All primary interactive elements — buttons, active navigation states, links, step indicators, progress bars, form focus rings.

---

#### Campaign Emerald — `#00C97A`

**RGB:** 0, 201, 122  
**HSL:** 152°, 100%, 39%

Emerald green carries strong positive resonance in MENA markets. In Arab cultural context, green is broadly associated with prosperity, growth, and positive momentum — not in a religious or flag-driven way, but at the level of intuitive color response. An emerald at this saturation level reads as modern and premium (not utilitarian), and its use as a success/growth signal aligns naturally with what users expect to see when a simulation delivers good news.

Beyond the regional angle, emerald functions as a clear semantic color for positive outcomes in any data platform. When engagement scores are up, when simulation completes successfully, when a variant wins — emerald confirms it. The human eye reads emerald-on-dark as "good result" without needing to read the label.

**Usage:** Simulation success states, positive metric deltas, upward trend indicators, completion badges, and KPI highlights on positive values.

---

#### Amber Gold — `#F0A500`

**RGB:** 240, 165, 0  
**HSL:** 41°, 100%, 47%

Gold and amber have deep aspirational associations in GCC culture. In the luxury and premium segment — which is large and influential in the region — gold signals quality and premium value. When applied to a data context (used by Bloomberg for positive financial data, and by many high-end analytics dashboards), amber/gold communicates that you are looking at **high-value information** — something worth your attention.

In CampaignSim's UI, Amber Gold is exclusively a data accent. It appears on key performance scores, resonance ratings, and premium insight callouts. Its scarcity makes it powerful: when the user sees amber, they know they are looking at something significant.

**Usage:** KPI score displays, "key insight" callouts in reports, top-performer badges, engagement rate highlights. Never used for interactive UI elements — only for data.

The dark theme is maintained from the existing implementation, but the undertone shifts entirely. Where the previous scheme used cold pure-black backgrounds, the new backgrounds carry a **barely perceptible teal trace** — enough to make the surfaces feel purposefully designed rather than default-dark, and to create natural harmony with the teal primary.

The teal trace in these backgrounds (`hue ~175°`) is intentional. On a calibrated display it is subtle enough to be invisible until the teal primary is placed on top of it — at which point the relationship between the surface and the UI element feels cohesive rather than pasted-on.

### 2.6 Color Usage Rules

1. **Teal is the action color.** If something is interactive, it is teal or it is ghost. There is no third interactive color.
2. **Amber is data-only.** Never use amber on a button, navigation item, or structural element. If you see amber, you are looking at a data value.
3. **Emerald means success.** Do not use emerald for decorative accents. When the user sees emerald, they should know a positive state has been reached.
4. **One accent per view.** Never show teal, emerald, and amber at equal visual weight on a single screen. Let one dominate based on context.
5. **No pure black, no pure white.** All surfaces use the scale above. All text uses the Polar/Haze/Mist/Ghost scale.
6. **No purple.** Under any circumstance. In any shade.

For hero sections, onboarding screens, and report covers:

    ellipse 70% 50% at 15% 60%,

Effect: A deep abyss with a faint teal aurora bottom-left and a barely visible emerald trace top-right. Communicates depth and intelligence without overwhelming the content placed on top.

## 3. Typography

### 3.0 Typography Strategy & Selection Rationale

Font selection is not aesthetic preference — it is a positioning decision. The typefaces a product uses communicate who it is before a single word is read. The following analysis explains both what was chosen and, critically, what was evaluated and rejected.

#### Why the Previous Fonts Were Replaced

The original CampaignSim implementation used **Syne** (display), **Plus Jakarta Sans** (body), and **Inter** (data). Each was individually competent but collectively they created a mismatch in brand signal:

- **Syne** at lighter weights reads as creative/editorial — appropriate for design agencies or art direction tools. At the weights used previously, it leaned toward "portfolio website" rather than "data intelligence platform." It also has limited number and symbol support, creating issues when used near metric displays.
- **Plus Jakarta Sans** is a rounded, approachable geometric sans-serif. Its character is "friendly B2B SaaS" — the same register as Figma, Loom, and Linear. That personality is too casual for a platform that produces authoritative, data-backed campaign predictions.
- **Inter** is technically excellent but has been adopted by approximately 40% of all SaaS products launched since 2020. Using Inter communicates nothing about brand identity — it is the visual equivalent of a blank slate. A platform that is genuinely differentiated at the product level should not be invisible at the typographic level.

#### The Selection Criteria

Replacements were evaluated against four criteria:
1. **Credibility register** — does the typeface feel appropriate for a professional analytics tool presenting predictive data to senior marketers?
2. **Distinctiveness** — is the typeface used by a meaningful percentage of direct comparables? If yes, eliminate it.
3. **Functional fit** — does it perform in the specific contexts required (dense data tables, KPI displays, academic documents, thesis slides)?
4. **Pairing coherence** — do the selected typefaces share a visual logic that makes them feel designed together, not assembled from different sources?

#### The Selected System: Space Grotesk + DM Sans + DM Mono

**Space Grotesk** (display/headings) replaces Syne. It was originally designed for technical and scientific communications — a heritage that directly mirrors CampaignSim's analytical identity. Its letterforms are geometrically precise with distinctive angled terminals that read as engineered, not decorative. Crucially, it is used in data analytics and fintech contexts globally (Cash App, Hashnode, various data dashboards) but has not yet become ubiquitous in the MarTech simulation space, making it a differentiating choice.

**DM Sans** (body/UI) replaces Plus Jakarta Sans. DM Sans was commissioned by DeepMind — an AI research laboratory — which gives it direct lineage to the AI tool category CampaignSim occupies. Its proportions are precisely calibrated for screen UI: slightly wider apertures than Inter for improved legibility at small sizes, less round than Plus Jakarta Sans for a more professional register. The AI heritage is a talking point, not a gimmick — it is documented and verifiable.

**DM Mono** (data/numbers) replaces Inter used for data. This is the most functionally important choice in the system. DM Mono was designed by the same foundry as DM Sans as an explicit companion — matching x-heights, consistent stroke weights, compatible visual rhythm. When a card shows a DM Sans label above a DM Mono value, the two elements read as a single designed unit. DM Mono also features tabular figures (all numerals are equal width), which is a critical feature for aligning decimal points and percentages in data tables — a failure mode visible in Inter-based implementations.

### 3.1 Philosophy

Three typefaces are used, each with a clearly defined role. The selection prioritizes legibility in data-dense UIs, professional authority for reports and academic presentations, and a clear aesthetic distinction between content types (display, body, data). All three are available free via Google Fonts and load efficiently as a paired set.

### 3.2 Display & Heading Font — **Space Grotesk**

```
Weights: 500, 600, 700
Role: Page titles, hero headlines, section headers, report titles, the logo wordmark
Source: fonts.google.com/specimen/Space+Grotesk
```

Space Grotesk is a geometric sans-serif with a critical quality: it was originally designed for technical and scientific publications. Its letterforms carry a structural precision — note the angled terminals, the mathematically consistent curves — that signals intelligence and rigor without feeling cold or corporate. It is now used extensively in data analytics, fintech, and AI products globally, but remains far less ubiquitous than Inter or Helvetica.

For CampaignSim, Space Grotesk anchors the brand in the data-science world. Its slightly heavier visual weight at 700 makes headlines read as statements, not questions. The `C`, `S`, and `M` in "CampaignSim" have particularly strong forms in Space Grotesk 700, making the wordmark distinctive at both large and small sizes.

**Type settings:**

### 3.3 Body & UI Font — **DM Sans**

```
Weights: 400, 500, 600
Role: All body copy, UI text, navigation labels, button text, form fields, tooltips
Source: fonts.google.com/specimen/DM+Sans
```

DM Sans (DeepMind Sans) was designed specifically for screen readability at small sizes. It was created by Colophon Foundry for DeepMind — an AI research lab — which makes it a defensible choice for an AI platform. Its proportions are balanced for UI contexts: not as narrow as Inter, not as wide as Outfit. The slightly open apertures improve legibility in dense data tables.

The specific reason DM Sans over Inter: Inter is now used by approximately 40% of all SaaS products. It is a sensible default, but it does not contribute anything to brand identity. DM Sans is equally legible, equally professional, and carries an AI-adjacent heritage that is appropriate for CampaignSim's positioning.

**Type settings:**
- Body: `400, 15px, line-height 1.65`
- UI labels: `500, 13px, line-height 1.5, letter-spacing 0.2px`
- Button text: `600, 14px, letter-spacing 0.3px`
- Navigation: `500, 14px`

### 3.4 Data & Monospace Font — **DM Mono**

```
Weights: 400, 500
Role: All numeric values, percentages, scores, simulation logs, KPI displays, code
Source: fonts.google.com/specimen/DM+Mono
```

DM Mono is the monospace companion to DM Sans — designed by the same foundry as a deliberate pairing. Using a companion monospace eliminates the visual friction that occurs when a geometric sans and an unrelated monospace typeface are mixed (differing x-heights, incompatible letter proportions). In practice, a KPI card showing a DM Sans label under a DM Mono value feels like a single designed object, not two pasted together.

DM Mono's tabular figures (all digits have equal width) make it ideal for aligning numbers in tables and charts. When scores like "87.4%" and "62.1%" stack vertically, the decimal points and digits align perfectly — a detail that matters enormously in a data-dense analytics interface.

**Type settings:**
- Large KPI: `500, 36px`
- Score display: `500, 24px`
- Inline metric: `500, 16px`
- Data table cell: `400, 14px`
- Log output: `400, 12px`

## 4. Logo Design

### 4.0 Logo Strategy & Concept Selection

#### Alternative Concepts Evaluated and Rejected

Before arriving at the final concept, three other directions were evaluated:

**Option considered: Network / Knowledge Graph Node**  
A hexagonal or circular node with radiating edges — representing the knowledge graph at the heart of the platform's architecture. This was rejected for two reasons. First, the "connected nodes" motif is now the default visual shorthand for "AI platform" and appears on thousands of product logos, dashboard illustrations, and conference slide decks. It is no longer distinctive. Second, and more importantly, it communicates the *mechanism* (knowledge graph) but not the *value* (predictive simulation before launch). The logo must communicate what the product *does for the user*, not how its internal architecture works.

**Option considered: Abstract geometric mark**  
A purely geometric symbol (overlapping shapes, a stylized letter form) disconnected from the product's function. Rejected because CampaignSim is not yet a brand with enough market recognition to sustain an abstract mark. Abstract logos work for companies that already have extensive awareness — Nike, Apple, Mercedes. A logo for a new platform must do functional work: it must communicate something about what the product does. An abstract geometric mark provides no cognitive entry point.

**Option considered: Waveform / Soundwave**  
A campaign "signal" visualized as a sine wave or waveform. This was close — the signal metaphor is correct — but waveforms are now strongly associated with audio technology (Spotify, SoundCloud, podcast apps, voice AI). Using a waveform would create category confusion, visually placing CampaignSim alongside audio tools rather than marketing analytics.

#### The Chosen Concept: "The Forecast Line"

The forecast line was selected because it passes a test that the other options failed: **every marketer immediately recognizes it as part of their own professional vocabulary.** It is not a new visual language they have to learn — it is the exact chart shape they look at every time they review campaign performance data. The solid-to-dashed transition, the rising arc, the arrowhead pointing toward a predicted future: this is the standard visual representation of a forecast in every analytics tool from Google Analytics to Meta Ads Manager to Tableau.

By compressing this shape into a logo mark, CampaignSim signals its category membership and its core value proposition simultaneously, with zero explanation required. The solid portion says: *we understand what already happened.* The dashed portion says: *we know what happens next — before it does.* The arrowhead says: *direction, momentum, forward.* This is the product, distilled to a single image.

### 4.1 Concept: "The Forecast Line"

The CampaignSim logo is built on a single visual metaphor: **the campaign performance curve with a simulated forecast extension.**

Every marketer has seen this shape. A line chart tracking campaign performance over time — impressions, clicks, conversions — and then, at the point of prediction, the line becomes dashed. The solid portion is what happened. The dashed portion is what the model expects to happen next. This is the visual language of forecasting, prediction, and simulation.

The CampaignSim mark takes this metaphor and compresses it into a symbol: a rising curve that is solid on the left (historical reality) and dashed on the right (simulated future), with an arrowhead terminal at the end of the dashed segment. Below the curve, a horizontal baseline. The whitespace between the curve and the baseline is shaped like the trajectory of growth — or the shape of a campaign gaining momentum.

This symbol is immediately legible to anyone who has ever looked at an analytics chart. It does not require explanation. It is the product.

---

### 4.2 Logo Option A — Symbol + Wordmark (Primary)

**The Symbol:**
- Canvas: 40×40px base unit (all measurements scale proportionally)
- A smooth arc rising from bottom-left to top-right, covering approximately 60% of canvas width
- Left 40% of the arc: solid stroke, 2.5px, Signal Teal (`#0ABFAD`)
- Right 60% of the arc: dashed stroke (8px dash / 4px gap), 2px, Signal Teal at 80% opacity
- Terminal arrowhead at the dashed end: 6px, filled Signal Teal
- Baseline: horizontal line 1px, Teal at 40% opacity, full width of canvas
- A vertical tick mark at the transition from solid to dashed: 4px height, 1px width, Teal at 60% — marks the "now" moment

**The Wordmark:**
Set in Space Grotesk 600. The name is written as two words with a mid-height separator:

```
Campaign·Sim
```

The center dot (`·`, Unicode interpunct, U+00B7) is set in Signal Teal (`#0ABFAD`). The text on both sides is Polar (`#EBF5F5`). This achieves three things: it visually separates "Campaign" from "Sim" without using a space or hyphen, it introduces the brand color into the typography, and it creates a subtle suggestion of the simulation "trigger point" — the moment the platform takes over.

**Lockup:**
Symbol sits 16px to the left of the wordmark, vertically centered with the cap height of the wordmark. At 28px type size, the symbol is 32px tall.

**On light backgrounds (thesis documents):**
Swap surface colors: symbol stroke becomes `#0ABFAD`, wordmark becomes `#070D0D`, separator dot stays `#0ABFAD`.

---

### 4.3 Logo Option B — Icon Only (App icon, favicon)

A square canvas with 14px rounded corners. Background fill: Signal Teal (`#0ABFAD`).

Inside: a white version of the forecast line mark at 72% of canvas width, centered. The arrowhead points to the upper-right corner. The baseline aligns with the bottom third of the canvas.

This variant works at 16×16px (favicon), 48×48px (app icon), 512×512px (store listing) without modification.

The use of teal as the fill rather than dark makes this icon highly visible on both dark app launchers and light ones — the teal background separates it from the content behind it at any size.

---

### 4.4 Logo Option C — Wordmark Only (Reports and Documentation)

For contexts where the symbol is too small or inappropriate — thesis cover page, PDF report header, academic bibliography:

```
CAMPAIGNSIM
```

Space Grotesk 700, all-caps, Polar (`#EBF5F5`) on dark or `#070D0D` on light. A 2px underline in Signal Teal begins at the `S` in SIM and extends to the final `M`. The underline sits 6px below the baseline.

For full black-and-white print: replace teal underline with 100% black, and set text to 100% black.

---

### 4.5 Clear Space Rule

The minimum clear space around any version of the logo is equal to the height of the capital `C` in the wordmark on all four sides. No competing typography, imagery, or UI elements enter this zone.

---

### 4.6 Logo Don'ts

- Do not rotate the forecast line symbol — directionality (left-to-right, rising) is load-bearing
- Do not use a teal background under the full wordmark lockup unless it is the icon-only version
- Do not use the symbol below 24px — the dash pattern and arrowhead disappear; use wordmark-only instead
- Do not use Option C in the product UI — it is for documents and print only
- Do not apply a drop shadow or outer glow to any logo variant
- Do not place any logo variant over a background with a teal gradient or teal-heavy pattern

## 13. Design Decision Summary

This section consolidates the reasoning behind every major brand decision in one place — for use in thesis defense, design critiques, or stakeholder presentations.

### 13.1 Why This Color Palette

**The fundamental question was:** what color family is available — not already owned by a dominant competitor, functionally relevant to marketing analytics, and culturally resonant in the primary target market?

The analysis eliminated:
- Cold blue → owned by enterprise software (Salesforce, LinkedIn, SAP)
- Purple/indigo → owned by creative tools and commoditized AI branding
- Orange as primary → consumer and FMCG associations, low professional credibility in the GCC B2B context
- Red → permanently reserved for error/danger semantics in any UI system

Teal was selected as the primary because it is the established color of data intelligence platforms globally, sits at the intersection of trust (blue) and growth (green), and is not yet owned by any platform in the pre-launch simulation or campaign intelligence category. It is used by Intercom, Zendesk, Atlassian, and Aether: Foundation — all credible, professional platforms whose positioning is adjacent but not identical to CampaignSim's.

Emerald green as the secondary success signal carries dual justification: it is the universal visual language for "positive outcome" in any analytics context, and green carries deep cultural associations with prosperity and growth in MENA markets. The emerald shade chosen (`#00C97A`) is saturated and modern — not the muted utility green of legacy enterprise software, and not the flat national-flag green of regional consumer brands.

Amber gold as the data accent is borrowed from financial analytics dashboards (Bloomberg's use of amber for positive financial data is the reference point) and calibrated to the GCC market's deeply-established association between gold and premium, high-value information. Its scarcity in the UI — reserved exclusively for data values, never for chrome or navigation — ensures that amber retains its premium signal value and does not become decorative.

The teal-green undertone across all background surfaces (`hue ~175°`) creates a cohesive relationship between the platform surfaces and the primary brand color. This is a deliberately subtle choice that distinguishes CampaignSim from platforms that use pure-black or cool-gray backgrounds by making the dark theme feel intentionally branded rather than default.

---

### 13.2 Why These Typefaces

**The fundamental problem with the previous type system:** Syne + Plus Jakarta Sans + Inter presented three typefaces that each communicated different brand registers. Syne signals editorial/creative. Plus Jakarta Sans signals friendly SaaS. Inter signals "we used the default." Together, they produced visual incoherence — the display type said "design tool," the body type said "startup app," and the data type said "generic."

**Space Grotesk** was selected as the display typeface because its origin — designed for technical and scientific publications — directly mirrors the analytical, data-driven register CampaignSim needs to communicate. It is used in data analytics and fintech contexts globally but remains non-ubiquitous in the MarTech space, making it a differentiating choice. Its geometric precision gives headlines the quality of statements rather than suggestions.

**DM Sans** was selected over Inter for a specific, evidence-based reason: DM Sans was commissioned by DeepMind, an AI research laboratory. In a platform whose core value proposition is AI-powered simulation, using a typeface with a documented AI heritage is a coherent, defensible decision — not a gimmick. It is equally legible as Inter but not shared by 40% of the SaaS market.

**DM Mono** is the single most technically important font choice in the system. Its selection over Inter for data display is not aesthetic — it is functional. DM Mono was designed as an explicit companion to DM Sans by the same foundry, meaning their x-heights, stroke weights, and optical spacing are calibrated to work together. When a metric label (DM Sans) appears above a metric value (DM Mono), they form a single visual unit. Additionally, DM Mono's tabular figures (fixed-width numerals) are essential for aligning decimal points and percentages in data tables — a failure mode that occurs with Inter's proportional figures in data-dense layouts.

---

### 13.3 Why This Logo

**The fundamental requirement** for a new platform logo: it must communicate what the product does without requiring explanation, work at favicon scale and on thesis cover pages simultaneously, and be distinctive enough to not be confused with any existing brand in the category.

The network node concept was rejected because it is the generic default for "AI platform" — thousands of logos already use it. It also communicates mechanism (how the product works internally) rather than value (what the product does for the user).

The forecast line was chosen because it is already part of the marketer's visual vocabulary. The solid-to-dashed line pattern is the universal representation of a forecast in every analytics tool a marketing professional uses daily. By compressing this metaphor into a logo mark, CampaignSim achieves two things simultaneously: it signals category membership (analytics/forecasting) and communicates its core differentiator (simulation and prediction before launch) — without a word of explanation. The arrowhead directs attention forward, reinforcing the brand positioning of being the tool you use *before* the campaign runs, not after.

The interpunct separator in the wordmark (`Campaign·Sim`) is a subtle reference to the "trigger point" — the moment in the logo mark where the solid line becomes dashed, the moment the platform takes over from historical data and begins predicting the future.
