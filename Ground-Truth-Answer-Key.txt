GROUND TRUTH ANSWER KEY — DO NOT UPLOAD TO CAMPAIGNSIM
=========================================================
This file exists ONLY for post-hoc human comparison after a simulation
has finished running. It must never be uploaded as a brief, pasted into
the brief text field, or otherwise given to the app — doing so would let
the knowledge-graph extraction and persona generation see the real
outcome, which defeats the entire point of a blind accuracy test.

Each brief file (Airbnb-Referral-Brief.txt, Dropbox-Referral-Brief.txt,
OldSpice-Viral-Brief.txt, CocaCola-ShareACoke-Brief.txt) now contains
ONLY brand context, audience segments, objective, and variants to test —
no outcome data. Use this file afterward to score how close the
simulation's predicted winner/metrics came to what actually happened.

DROPBOX REFERRAL PROGRAM
=========================================================
Per Dropbox's own widely published growth figures (Drew Houston,
founder/CEO, in multiple public talks and retrospectives), the referral
program was one of the single largest contributors to user growth
during this period: referrals accounted for roughly 35% of all daily
signups at peak, and the program is credited with permanently
increasing the overall signup rate by approximately 60% after launch —
while also cutting customer acquisition cost relative to paid
advertising. Dropbox grew from roughly 100,000 registered users in
September 2008 to roughly 4,000,000 registered users by December 2009
(commonly cited as ~3900% growth in 15 months). By April 2010, users
had sent a cumulative 2.8 million direct referral invites. The mechanic
(give storage, get storage) outperformed cash-based incentive tests
Dropbox ran internally, because the reward was the same currency as the
product itself.

Check: does the simulation predict strong referral participation /
high engagement with the storage-based incentive relative to a
hypothetical cash incentive?

OLD SPICE "THE MAN YOUR MAN COULD SMELL LIKE"
=========================================================
The original campaign goal was a 15% increase in body wash sales. The
actual results, per published case studies and marketing press
coverage, far exceeded that: by May 2010, Old Spice Red Zone Body Wash
sales were up 60% year-over-year; by July 2010, sales had doubled; by
the end of July 2010, sales were up 125% year-over-year, and by the end
of 2010 Old Spice had become the #1 selling men's body wash brand in
the United States.

On video performance: the original "Man Your Man Could Smell Like" spot
reached more than 10 million YouTube views within weeks (reportedly
more than 10x the views of that year's Dove Super Bowl ad) and over 40
million views within its first 30 days. The Response Campaign that
followed generated more than 65 million views in total and is widely
cited as one of the fastest-growing and most-viewed interactive ad
campaigns in online video history at the time. During the campaign
period, Old Spice's Twitter following grew approximately 2,700%,
Facebook fans grew approximately 800% (roughly 500,000 to 800,000), and
YouTube subscribers more than doubled (roughly 65,000 to 150,000). Old
Spice is reported to have captured 76% of online conversation/buzz in
its category in the three months following launch — over half of it
from women, the campaign's primary intended audience.

Check: does the simulation predict the interactive Response campaign
(Variant B) drives higher engagement/amplification than the one-way
spot (Variant A), consistent with the real campaign's heavier weighting
toward interactive virality?

COCA-COLA "SHARE A COKE" (AUSTRALIA, 2011)
=========================================================
Per published case studies, in the first Australian summer of the
campaign (2011–2012), Coca-Cola volume sales rose by approximately 4%,
snapping a decade-long decline in a single season, and young adult
consumption specifically rose by approximately 7%. Coca-Cola sold
roughly 250 million personalized bottles and cans in Australia — a
country of approximately 23 million people at the time, meaning the
average Australian bought roughly ten personalized Cokes. On social
media, Facebook traffic in Australia rose approximately 870% during
the campaign's initial six-month run, and more than 500,000 photos were
shared using #ShareACoke in the first year. The campaign was judged
successful enough that Coca-Cola expanded the personalized-name
mechanic globally in subsequent years, eventually running it in more
than 70 countries.

Check: does the simulation predict the personalization mechanic
(Variants B/C) outperforms a generic promotional push (Variant A) among
the younger, disengaged segment specifically?

E.L.F. COSMETICS "EYES. LIPS. FACE." (2019)
=========================================================
Per published case studies and trade coverage (AdExchanger, Adweek,
Campaign US, WARC), the #EyesLipsFace hashtag challenge on TikTok
became the most viral branded campaign in TikTok US history at the
time: roughly 5 million user-generated videos, totaling around 7
billion views, and the fastest campaign ever to hit 1 billion views
(in 6 days). The original song reached #4 on Spotify's global chart.
On Instagram, the brand also saw a real but far smaller secondary
lift: engagement rose approximately 250% within 60 days of launch.
Paid performance also improved (56% reduction in cost-per-acquisition,
1.9x conversion CTR vs. benchmark). e.l.f. rose from the #8 to #2
favorite beauty brand among teens during this period.

Check: does the simulation predict the TikTok short-form variant
(Variant A) decisively outperforms the Instagram video variant
(Variant B) — consistent with TikTok being the overwhelming primary
driver and Instagram a real but much smaller secondary effect?

Social Insider, Brand24, Marketing Brew), Duolingo's TikTok account is

language-learning apps on TikTok. By comparison, Duolingo's Instagram
account, while healthy, runs at a markedly lower average engagement
rate (reported around 4%). Duolingo's own social strategy explicitly
treats TikTok as the chaotic, discovery-oriented short-form channel,
and Instagram as the more curated, meme/carousel channel for an
existing-follower audience.

SURREAL CEREAL — LINKEDIN BRAND STRATEGY (UK, ongoing)
=========================================================
Per multiple independent trade case studies (Creative Salon, Behavio,
The Scaleup Collective, OptiMonk, Whito, Marketing Monk, Technology
for Marketing), Surreal deliberately built its primary brand following
on LinkedIn — an highly unusual channel choice for a consumer food
brand — by posting irreverent, advertising-industry-insider content
designed to be shared by marketers and agency people specifically.
This unconventional bet paid off: Surreal grew to roughly 110,000+
LinkedIn followers, larger than its Instagram following of roughly
69,000–112,000 (reported figures vary slightly by source/date), making
LinkedIn its standout, most-talked-about channel despite being a
B2C food brand. The strategy is widely credited as a major driver of
Surreal becoming the fastest-growing independent cereal brand in the
UK, reaching over 3,500 retail stores.

Check: does the simulation predict the LinkedIn industry-insider
variant (Variant A) outperforms the Instagram product-carousel variant
(Variant B), consistent with LinkedIn being Surreal's unexpectedly
strongest channel?
