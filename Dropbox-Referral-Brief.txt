DROPBOX REFERRAL PROGRAM — CAMPAIGN BRIEF
Prepared for: Marketing Campaign Simulation Validation
Source: Published Dropbox growth case studies (Drew Houston, Y Combinator Startup School talks; widely cited growth-hacking retrospectives, 2008–2010)

=== BRAND OVERVIEW ===

Dropbox is a cloud file-storage and synchronization service that lets users save files online and access them from any device. In its early years (2008–2010), Dropbox's entire growth strategy centered on product-led virality rather than paid acquisition — the product itself was built to spread.

Brand values: Simplicity, reliability, "it just works."
Market positioning: The effortless way to keep your files everywhere, with no setup friction.

=== THE REFERRAL PROGRAM ===

Dropbox's referral program is a double-sided storage incentive. An existing user invites a friend via a unique referral link; once the friend signs up and installs Dropbox, both the referrer and the new user receive bonus storage space (initially 250MB per successful referral, later 500MB, up to a capped maximum of additional free storage).

Unlike a cash incentive, the reward is the product itself — more of the exact thing the user already wants more of. This made the incentive feel native rather than transactional, and removed any "selling out a friend for money" friction.

=== TARGET AUDIENCE SEGMENTS ===

Segment 1 — The Early Adopter Power User
Age: 22–34.
Psychographics: Technically literate, already juggling files across multiple devices, frustrated by emailing files to themselves. Active in tech-forward online communities.
Platforms: Email, tech forums/communities, word-of-mouth among coworkers.
Buying behaviour: Adopts new tools early, evangelizes tools that solve a real daily pain point.
Ad response: Unmoved by traditional advertising. Highly responsive to in-product prompts that show a concrete, immediate personal benefit (e.g., "get more space").

Segment 2 — The Collaborative Team Member
Age: 25–45.
Psychographics: Works on shared documents/projects, frequently needs to send large files to colleagues or classmates, mildly frustrated by email attachment size limits.
Platforms: Email, in-product notifications, workplace/team chat mentions.
Buying behaviour: Adopts tools that a colleague already uses, to reduce friction in collaboration.
Ad response: Responds to social proof ("your colleague uses this") and to reminders tied to a real moment of need (e.g., right after hitting a storage limit).

=== CAMPAIGN OBJECTIVE ===

Objective: Drive referral invites sent and completed (friend signs up + installs) among existing free-tier Dropbox users.

Context: Dropbox is testing where in the product experience a referral prompt is surfaced, to see which placement converts existing users into active referrers most effectively.

Target metric: Number of completed referrals (successful friend signups) per 1,000 active free-tier users exposed to the prompt.

=== CAMPAIGN VARIANTS TO TEST ===

VARIANT A — "Sponsored Workflow Tip" (professional-context placement)
Headline: Stop emailing files to yourself.
Body: Power users juggle files across a laptop, tablet, and phone — and waste time doing it. Invite a teammate to Dropbox and you'll both get 500MB of extra space, free.
CTA: Invite a teammate
Target segment: Early Adopter Power User (Segment 1)
Channel: LinkedIn
Format: SponsoredPost

VARIANT B — "Email Re-Engagement Nudge" (sent to existing users who have not yet referred anyone)
Headline: Get more space. It's free.
Body: You've got friends who could use more storage too. Send them a Dropbox invite and you'll both get 500MB extra, free.
CTA: Send an invite
Target segment: Collaborative Team Member (Segment 2)
Channel: Email
Format: EmailNewsletter

=== BRAND VOICE ===

Tone: Plain, friendly, low-key. No hype, no urgency tactics.
We focus on the concrete, immediate benefit (more space) rather than abstract brand storytelling.
